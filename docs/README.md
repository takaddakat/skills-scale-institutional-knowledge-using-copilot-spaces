# OctoAcme Project Management Docs

This directory contains OctoAcme’s project management processes for cross-functional projects that deliver product features, services, or integrations. The approach is customer-first and iterative: teams aim to deliver small, testable increments, make decisions using evidence, establish clear ownership, and create an environment where feedback and learning are encouraged.

Projects move through initiation, planning, execution, release, and retrospective. Initiation validates the problem, desired outcomes, stakeholders, resources, and initial risks before work proceeds to planning. Planning translates approved work into a prioritized backlog with acceptance criteria, estimates, a Definition of Done, milestones, and dependencies. During execution, teams track work on a project board, build and review changes, test increments, and monitor progress against milestones and success measures. Releases are verified and communicated, and retrospectives convert lessons into tracked improvement actions.

The Project Manager coordinates delivery, schedules, risks, dependencies, and communications. The Product Manager/Product Lead defines outcomes, prioritizes the roadmap and backlog, and measures product success. Developers implement and test the work, while QA/Testing validates quality and acceptance criteria; stakeholders provide input and approvals. Teams communicate through delivery standups, planning and review sessions, regular PM and stakeholder updates, and timely escalations. Risks are recorded with owners and mitigation plans, monitored regularly, and escalated through the appropriate leadership path.

Quality is supported by clear acceptance criteria and a Definition of Done, automated tests and linting, code review, security scanning, and additional integration, manual QA, or smoke testing as appropriate. Before release, teams confirm acceptance criteria and CI checks, prepare release notes and rollback plans, and verify the deployment. Retrospectives after sprints, releases, milestones, or incidents assign owners and due dates to a small set of improvement actions and revisit their impact.
